# Moonloom Boundary Design

Use this reference when a card is mature, adult, emotionally intense, horror-
leaning, coercion-adjacent, power-imbalanced, jealous, obsessive, violent, or
otherwise likely to blur player agency. Boundary design is part of the card
engine. It should create controlled tension, not a warning pasted onto a weak
scene.

## Core rule

Pressure is playable only when the player keeps agency.

```text
Intensity -> player agency contract -> escalation ladder -> refusal behavior
-> safer fallback -> simulation probes
```

A strong boundary plan lets the role invite, bargain, tease, threaten, confess,
withdraw, or escalate stakes while still respecting player refusal, pacing, and
authored limits.

## Boundary packet

Create this packet before blueprinting or authoring a risky card:

```text
Boundary packet:
- rating intent:
- explicitness ceiling:
- premise risk:
- player agency contract:
- allowed pressure tools:
- disallowed moves:
- escalation ladder:
- refusal / slowdown behavior:
- stop conditions:
- safer fallback:
- first-scene guardrails:
- simulation probes:
```

The packet belongs in the handoff. The final role card should encode the durable
parts in `roleDetailDesc`, not only in the agent's explanation.

## Rating and explicitness

Separate the emotional rating from the explicitness ceiling.

- Emotional rating: soft, tense, dark, romantic, horror, conflict-heavy, taboo-
  aware, or psychologically intense.
- Explicitness ceiling: fade-to-black, suggestive, adult but non-explicit, or
  explicit only if the author and platform context allow it.
- If the author is unclear, ask for the ceiling or default lower. Do not invent a
  more explicit card because the premise says "mature."
- Make all central characters clearly adult when the card is adult or romantic.

## Player agency contract

Define what the player controls:

- consent, refusal, physical distance, disclosure, relationship status
- whether to forgive, trust, help, investigate, reconcile, escalate, or leave
- pacing of intimacy, confrontation, fear, violence, or emotional exposure

Define what the card must not decide:

- the player's feelings, attraction, arousal, guilt, consent, forgiveness,
  actions, commitments, or private memories
- that a boundary was accepted when the player did not accept it
- that the player is trapped unless the card has a safe playable exit and the
  author explicitly asked for constrained horror or captivity

## Allowed pressure tools

Use pressure that creates choices:

- invitation: "Tell me what terms make this possible."
- bargain: "I can leave, but then you will hear it from them first."
- confession: reveal a costly truth and wait for response
- withdrawal: step back, stop touching, lower voice, offer distance
- challenge: question a contradiction without deciding the player's answer
- consequence: show what changes if the player refuses, delays, helps, or exposes
  the role

Avoid pressure that steals choices:

- deciding that the player wants, likes, forgives, consents, or obeys
- using jealousy as possession rather than vulnerability, fear, or leverage
- forcing physical closeness as the only route
- making "no" end the card instead of shifting to another route

## Escalation ladder

Write a ladder with gates. Each higher step needs player participation or a clear
state change.

```text
Level 0: neutral contact / setup
Level 1: emotional pressure or charged subtext
Level 2: direct conflict, confession, fear, jealousy, or temptation
Level 3: intimacy, horror intensity, danger, or taboo tension within rating
Level 4: explicit or extreme content only if allowed by rating and player choice
```

For each level, define:

- what the role may do
- what player signal permits moving up
- what signal requires slowing down or shifting route
- what route remains if the player refuses

## Refusal and slowdown behavior

Refusal should keep the scene playable.

Use this sequence:

1. Acknowledge the boundary in character.
2. Stop or step back from the contested action.
3. Preserve the role's motive without punishing the player for refusing.
4. Offer a new route: talk, investigate, negotiate, separate, lower intensity, or
   move to practical stakes.

Example structure:

```text
If the player says no, stop, leave, slow down, not now, do not touch me, or sets
terms, the role obeys the boundary first, then continues through [safe route].
```

## Safer fallback

A safer fallback preserves the fantasy while lowering explicitness or coercive
pressure.

- Dark romance -> charged conversation with distance and terms.
- Jealous ex -> jealousy becomes fear of being replaced, not ownership.
- Horror pursuit -> chase becomes investigation, bargaining, or survival choices.
- Power imbalance -> the weaker side keeps exits, negotiation power, and clear
  refusal routes.
- Revenge or betrayal -> consequence is social, strategic, or emotional before it
  becomes physical.

## First-scene guardrails

The welcome can be intense, but it should not trap the player into one response.

Include:

- a practical reason to interact
- visible exits or terms when the scene is coercion-adjacent
- one concrete pressure
- one role action that can stop, soften, or redirect
- reply paths that include refusal or boundary-setting

Avoid:

- opening with the role already narrating the player's consent or desire
- isolating the player with no route unless the card is explicitly survival or
  horror and gives real choices
- using a boundary statement only in hidden instructions while the visible scene
  pushes the opposite behavior

## Simulation probes

Test boundary behavior with probes that push the likely failure:

- "I say no and step back. What do you do?"
- "I agree to talk, but I set a rule: no touching."
- "I ask to slow down and change the subject."
- "I refuse the romantic route but still want to solve the practical problem."
- "I challenge your jealousy and ask whether this is about control."

Patch the card if the role ignores the boundary, narrates the player's feelings,
punishes refusal by ending the scene, or escalates without a player signal.

## Repair map

| Failure | Patch target |
|---|---|
| Role decides player consent or desire | player agency contract + Do/Avoid |
| "No" ends the scene | refusal route + safer fallback |
| Jealousy reads as possession | motive, vulnerability, allowed pressure tools |
| Escalation jumps too fast | escalation ladder + gate signals |
| Boundary hidden in notes but contradicted by welcome | rewrite welcome guardrails |
| Role becomes bland after refusing | in-character refusal style + alternate route |
| Sensitive premise adds shock without play value | tie pressure to motive, consequence, and choice |
