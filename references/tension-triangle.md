# Moonloom Tension Triangle

Use this reference when a role-card idea has attractive mood, character, setting,
or profile promise but still feels inert: nothing forces the first scene to start,
the player has little leverage, or the role has no active desire.

The tension triangle is:

```text
role desire -> player leverage -> external pressure -> why now -> first move
```

A strong card should be able to answer:

- What does the role want enough to act now?
- What can the player decide, withhold, reveal, risk, change, spend, or refuse?
- What external pressure makes the scene start now rather than someday?
- What is the consequence if the player does nothing?

Do not solve inert premises with prettier prose. Add a playable pressure system.

## When To Use

Use tension weaving when:

- the premise is pretty but passive
- the card has a role and setting but no "why now"
- the opening is likely to become greeting, mood, or exposition
- the player can admire, comfort, or watch but cannot change pressure
- the world, relationship, or mystery has facts but no immediate cost
- the author asks for stakes, hook, pressure, conflict, urgency, or a stronger
  reason to open before fields

Route away when:

- the author has only loose taste signals and no chosen direction: use premise
  workshop first
- the role itself is trope-only or has no desire/boundary: use character core
- the player is overwritten or routes are decorative: use agency design
- the first screen is already the only weak layer: use opening director
- the issue is scenario clue pacing, game mechanics, relationship pacing, or
  daily-life routine structure: use that narrow engine first

## Tension Sources

Pick one or two sources. Do not stack every possible conflict.

| Source | Works when | Example pressure function |
|---|---|---|
| Secret | knowledge changes access, trust, or risk | player can hide, expose, test, bargain |
| Debt | someone owes or is owed something | player can collect, forgive, delay, transfer |
| Timer | time changes state or closes a route | player chooses what to risk before deadline |
| Boundary | a line can be crossed, defended, or renegotiated | player can set terms or push back |
| Resource | something scarce affects future choices | player can spend, save, steal, trade |
| Social risk | public attention creates cost | player can cover, accuse, perform, withdraw |
| Rule | a world/system rule creates consequence | player can exploit, obey, break, reveal |
| Care cost | helping one thing neglects another | player can choose who or what changes |

## Quality Checks

- Role desire creates action, not only personality.
- Player leverage changes consequence, not only mood.
- External pressure has a visible first-scene form.
- "Why now" can be proven in one welcome beat.
- If the player stays passive, the role has a next move that preserves agency.
- Reply paths lead to different state, route, access, risk, relationship, clue,
  or boundary outcomes.
- The packet does not decide the player's feelings, consent, loyalty, actions,
  or final route.
- Token cost is paid by replacing vague setup with concrete pressure, not by
  adding a second lore layer.

## Handoff

- Use `lunatalk-character-core` when role desire or boundary is missing.
- Use `lunatalk-agency-designer` when the player has no real control or is
  overwritten.
- Use `lunatalk-opening-director` when the triangle exists and needs first-screen
  execution.
- Use `lunatalk-scenario-architect`, `lunatalk-play-engineer`,
  `lunatalk-relationship-architect`, or `lunatalk-daily-life-architect` when the
  pressure belongs to a specialized engine.
- Use `lunatalk-card-blueprint` when the tension packet is ready for broader
  blueprinting.
