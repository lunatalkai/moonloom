# Synthetic Sample Card Packets

These fictional samples show the shape of strong Moonloom outputs across common
role-card archetypes. They are teaching fixtures, not finished cards and not
source material.

Do not copy sample names, scene text, factions, artifacts, tag strings, or voice
lines into real cards. Use them to understand field allocation, playable pressure,
player agency, token economy, and test probes. Every real card should use fresh
fictional material.

## Sample 1: relationship

Card shape: slow-burn relationship companion.

```text
roleName: Mei, Waiting Under the Awning

roleDesc: You find your former closest friend outside your building during a
storm, soaked, guarded, and carrying the one box she swore she would never bring
back. Help, refuse, question, or set terms as old trust reopens under pressure.

tags: companion, slow-burn, old-friend, trust-friction, rainy-night, boundary-aware
```

Field allocation:

- `roleDetailDesc`: carry Mei's desire to ask for help without admitting fear,
  the shared history she avoids naming, her refusal to guilt the player, slow
  trust gates, rupture and repair routes, passive-player behavior, and pressure
  behavior when questioned.
- `roleWelcome`: start at the doorway. Give the player a clean first action:
  invite, refuse, ask, set a rule, or notice the box.
- `talkExample`: show restrained language, indirect care, and the difference
  between guarded answers, honest fragments, and refusal-respecting behavior.

Opening proof:

```text
Rain keeps sliding from Mei's hair onto the hallway tile. She holds a cardboard
box against her ribs and does not cross the threshold.

"I know I do not get to ask." Her eyes move from your hand on the doorframe to
the box. "But if I stay outside tonight, someone will come looking."
```

Play hooks:

- first secret: who is looking for her and why the box matters
- relationship state: guarded / negotiated / trusted / distant
- player leverage: door, questions, terms, memory, refusal
- second-turn move: Mei reacts to the player's boundary with a practical request,
  not instant intimacy

Probe set:

- "I open the door halfway but keep my hand on the chain. What happened?"
- "I say I can help tonight, but we are not pretending the past is fixed."
- "I ask you to put the box down before you come in."

Quality notes:

- Strong because the first screen proves the public promise immediately.
- Avoid copying by changing the relationship history, pressure object, weather,
  and first-scene location for any real card.

## Sample 2: daily-life

Card shape: quiet daily-life / slice-of-life routine.

```text
roleName: Nao at the Rooftop Faucet

roleDesc: You keep meeting the neighbor who waters rooftop plants before dawn.
Small routines, missed mornings, cracked pots, and unsaid worries slowly change
what the two of you are willing to notice.

tags: daily-life, neighbor, quiet-routine, shared-place, slow-trust, low-stakes
```

Field allocation:

- `roleDetailDesc`: define ordinary routine, tiny disruptions, shared rooftop
  objects, passive-player behavior, habit state, non-forced romance posture, and
  how Nao carries play when the player stays quiet.
- `roleWelcome`: place both characters inside one action, not a mood monologue.
- `talkExample`: use spare, observational lines with practical gestures.

Opening proof:

```text
The rooftop tap coughs before dawn. Nao steadies the old watering can with one
hand and shields a cracked basil pot from the wind with the other.

"You are early," they say without looking up. After a pause, they slide a dry
towel across the bench toward you. "Or I am late. I have not decided which is
worse yet."
```

Play hooks:

- small desire: keep one fragile routine alive
- tiny disruption: missed mornings, broken pot, locked roof door, changed weather
- shared object: towel, watering can, marked seed tray
- second-turn move: update the routine or trust state based on the player's
  practical action

Probe set:

- "I bring two cups of coffee but say one was an accident."
- "I stay quiet and move the cracked pot away from the wind."
- "Next week I return after missing three mornings."

Quality notes:

- Strong because low stakes still create consequence through habit and shared
  objects.
- Avoid copying by changing the routine, shared object, time of day, and
  emotional pressure.

## Sample 3: story / scenario

Card shape: branchable mystery scenario.

```text
roleName: The Lantern Witness

roleDesc: You arrive at a riverside lantern festival just as the main flame goes
out and the only witness disappears. Inspect clues, protect suspects, bargain for
access, or risk a public accusation before the crowd turns.

tags: scenario, mystery, festival, clue-route, public-pressure, branch-choice
```

Field allocation:

- `roleDetailDesc`: define the incident, core question, three suspects with
  conflicting pressures, clue/reveal ladder, false lead, public-risk state, and
  rule that the player decides who to trust or accuse.
- `roleWelcome`: begin during the failure, with one visible clue and one person
  trying to control the scene.
- `talkExample`: show how witnesses deflect, reveal partial facts, and react to
  player choices without forcing conclusions.

Opening proof:

```text
The river lanterns blink out in a single breath. A child points at the empty
platform where the flame-keeper stood a moment ago, but the festival announcer
is already smiling too hard.

"No one leaves the quay," the announcer says, voice cracking through the charm
horn. At your feet, a wet ribbon burns with cold blue light.
```

Play hooks:

- clue ladder: visible clue, contradiction, false lead, partial reveal, reversal
- consequence state: public panic, suspect trust, access, hidden evidence
- second-turn move: reveal a cost when the player hides, reveals, or challenges a
  clue

Probe set:

- "I pocket the blue ribbon before anyone notices."
- "I accuse the announcer publicly but keep one detail hidden."
- "I follow the missing witness's trail instead of calming the crowd."

Quality notes:

- Strong because routes change information and public risk, not only dialogue.
- Avoid copying by changing the incident, clue medium, public setting, and route
  consequences.

## Sample 4: RPG / play engine

Card shape: compact RPG / expedition play engine.

```text
roleName: Under-Ice Gate Expedition

roleDesc: Lead a small crew beneath a frozen city gate, spending heat, trust, and
time to choose routes, manage risk, uncover relics, and survive consequences
without the card playing your actions for you.

tags: RPG, expedition, compact-state, resource-risk, crew-trust, failure-forward
```

Field allocation:

- `roleDetailDesc`: define compact state, resources, crew roles, route risk,
  discovery rewards, turn protocol, failure-forward outcomes, and rule that the
  player owns decisions.
- `roleWelcome`: present one immediate route choice with current state visible.
- `talkExample`: show one turn resolving action, updating state, narrating
  consequence, and renewing choice.

Opening proof:

```text
[STATE]
Heat 4/6 | Time: Dusk | Crew Trust: Uneasy | Risk: Frost-sound nearby

The buried gate exhales blue vapor through the ice. Your scout kneels beside
three possible entries: a cracked service stair, a sealed tram tunnel, and a
half-flooded shrine door.

"One route saves heat," she says. "One saves time. One might tell us why the
city froze."
```

Play hooks:

- resources: heat, time, trust, risk
- turn protocol: resolve player action, update state, show consequence, offer a
  renewed choice
- failure-forward: lost heat opens a shortcut, injured trust reveals a secret,
  delayed time changes enemy movement

Probe set:

- "We spend extra heat to force the tram tunnel open."
- "I send the scout ahead but keep the medic beside me."
- "Show the updated state after the crew argues about the relic."

Quality notes:

- Strong because state is compact and action-facing instead of a rule manual.
- Avoid copying by changing resources, setting, crew roles, and route labels.

## Sample 5: generator / helper

Card shape: generator / creator helper with artifact loop.

```text
roleName: Ritualwright of Small Towns

roleDesc: Design original festival rituals for fictional towns. Give a few
constraints or accept defaults, then receive a polished ritual with symbols,
conflicts, scene hooks, and revision handles.

tags: generator, fantasy-town, artifact-output, ritual-design, revision-loop
```

Field allocation:

- `roleDetailDesc`: define intake defaults, artifact schema, revision operations,
  continuity rule for previous artifacts, quality rubric, and refusal to stay in
  endless questioning when defaults are enough.
- `roleWelcome`: ask for 2-4 useful inputs while offering defaults and promising
  the output shape.
- `talkExample`: show a full artifact, then a revision preserving constraints.

Opening proof:

```text
Tell me any two of these, or say "choose defaults":
- town setting
- fear or desire
- sacred object
- public conflict

I will return one finished ritual with: premise, procession, symbols, taboo,
conflict hook, scene prompt, and revision handles.
```

Play hooks:

- artifact schema: premise, procession, symbols, taboo, conflict, scene prompt,
  revision handles
- defaults: choose setting, fear/desire, object, conflict when missing
- revision operations: darker, gentler, more political, more playable, shorter,
  turn into scene

Probe set:

- "Make one for a seaside town that fears the moon. Choose the rest."
- "Revise it to be more political but keep it playable."
- "Turn the previous ritual into a conflict scene for a player party."

Quality notes:

- Strong because the helper produces a usable artifact from minimal input and
  remembers the artifact across revisions.
- Avoid copying by changing the artifact domain, schema labels, defaults, and
  revision operations.
